Overview

Seven messages from the third generation of NDCs.

    Message 01    Presence

    Is transport getting more attention?

    Transport has arrived.

    This is the generation with the most transport content yet. Countries responsible for 77% of global transport emissions submitted an NDC, and 9 out of 10 describe transport through an action or a target.

    Explore hover or tap any line

    The 55% counts every country with a transport mitigation target, whether it is measured in emissions, vehicles, fuel or mode share.

    On the 77%: international flights and ships produce 16% of transport emissions and belong to no single country, so no NDC can target them. That makes 84%, not 100%, the most any set of national plans could ever cover.

    Message 02    Targets

    Does a target guarantee a real cut in emissions?

    Most targets allow emissions to keep growing.

    Thirty six countries set a transport emissions reduction target. Most measure it against projected growth rather than a past year, so the target can be met while emissions still rise.

    Explore hover or tap any line

    A business as usual target is measured against a forecast of what emissions would have been without action. It can be met while emissions still rise. A base year target is measured against a real past year, so meeting it requires a real reduction.

    Conditional targets are an expected part of the Paris framework, and many countries state openly which part of their goal depends on international finance. It is reported here as a fact about delivery, not about ambition.

    Message 03    Biggest emitters

    Are the targets where the emissions are?

    The targets sit where the emissions are not.

    Targets exist, but not where the emissions are. The countries that set one are largely the smaller and more vulnerable economies, while most of the largest emitters commit to measures instead.

    Explore hover or tap any line

    The European Union counts as one of the 126 NDCs and one of the 36 targets throughout this brochure, not as 27 separate countries. Its target sets CO2 standards for road vehicles, not a target for transport as a whole sector. Treated as a group, its emissions rank it third in the world.

    Message 04    Electrification

    How do countries plan to reach their targets?

    Electrification, the favourite action, rarely detailed.

    Electrification appears in more NDCs than any other approach. Two thirds of its actions name no instrument, no incentive, standard or programme, only the intention. The measures that would change the system rather than the vehicle stay rare.

    Explore hover or tap any line

    Naming an instrument means stating how the change happens: a purchase incentive, an emissions standard, a charging programme, a procurement rule. An action without one records an intention rather than a plan.

    Electrification also reaches further than the measures that would change the system rather than the vehicle. Shifting trips to other modes appears in 89 NDCs, and walking and cycling in 33.

    Message 05    Freight

    Goods move a large share of the emissions. Do the NDCs move with them?

    Six NDCs name freight in a target.

    Goods move 43% of transport emissions. Only six NDCs set a target that names freight, and none of them is measured in emissions.

    Explore hover or tap any line

    Two examples: Moldova: electric urban freight by 2040. Nepal: 300 km of electric freight rail by 2035.

    The 43% figure is a 2023 measure that includes international shipping and aviation, sourced from Kuehne Climate Center and SLOCAT, 2024.

    Message 06    People

    Transport is the sector climate policy touches most directly in daily life. Who is it planned for?

    91 of 126 NDCs name no one.

    Searched in English, Spanish and French across every transport action, target and stated benefit. Twenty five NDCs name a social group. Fifteen reference gender. No transport target does.

    Explore hover or tap any line

    Most references are descriptive. Kenya promises transport that is gender responsive; Pakistan attaches the phrase to a bus corridor. A few are operational: Bangladesh commits to women and disability friendly public transport policies, Benin to solar tricycles for women traders, and the Maldives is the only NDC to identify a gendered climate impact, noting that disrupted sea transport between islands cuts access to maternal health care.

    Message 07    Adaptation

    How are countries planning to handle the impacts already arriving?

    Adaptation mostly means stronger roads.

    Eighty NDCs write about adapting transport. Six in ten of those actions are structural, and most of those reinforce existing roads and bridges rather than change how networks are planned, maintained or operated.

    Explore hover or tap any line

    Structural actions build or reinforce physical assets. The alternative is planning where roads and railways are routed, maintaining what exists, and operating networks differently when floods or heat arrive. Those appear far less often.

    Adaptation is now widely written about: 80 of 126 NDCs include transport adaptation content, up from 20% in the first generation. Presence is not the gap. Commitment is.

    Mitigation targets in these NDCs routinely reach 2050. No transport adaptation target extends past 2035, a shorter horizon than the infrastructure it is meant to protect.

    Conclusion

    Is transport on track for 1.5°C?

    Not yet

    These NDCs point to 2.3 to 2.5°C of warming. Transport needs to reduce emissions by at least 28% below 2019 levels by 2035 to reach the 1.5°C target.

    Sources and limitations.

    Sources

    GIZ and SLOCAT NDC Transport Tracker, export of August 2026, covering the 126 active third generation NDCs. Six countries filed a revision and only the current version counts. Emissions are taken from EDGAR 2024.

    What counts as a third generation NDC

    We consider all NDC submissions made since November 2024 to be part of the third generation of NDCs, regardless of the document's title or version number. The European Union counts as one submission covering 27 countries. The findings may therefore differ from those of other NDC 3.0 trackers.

    What an NDC does not cover

    An NDC is not a country's full transport agenda. It records what a government chose to put in this document, not everything it plans, funds or already does. A country may be acting on transport without that action appearing here.

    What sits outside any NDC

    International shipping and aviation belong to no single country and fall outside the scope of every NDC. They account for 16% of global transport emissions and are reported separately throughout.
